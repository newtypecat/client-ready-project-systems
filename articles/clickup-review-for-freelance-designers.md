### What this review is judging

A freelance designer does not need "project management" in the abstract. The tool has to do three specific jobs:

1. Give each client a place to see their project's status — and nothing else.
2. Make scope visible, so extra work becomes a recorded change instead of a silent expectation.
3. Track time on the same tasks the client sees, so an invoice rests on records, not memory.

Article 1 on this site compared ClickUp with monday.com. This review looks at ClickUp alone, in the order a freelancer would set it up: the client-facing portal first, then the guest rules that decide what clients can actually do, then time tracking, then the bill.

### Quick verdict

- **ClickUp fits** if you are solo or a team of 1–3, you bill by the hour or by the project, and you want client visibility without paying for a separate portal tool. The paid Core plan is $9 per user/month billed yearly (official pricing page, checked November 27, 2026), and view-only client access is unlimited on paid plans.
- **ClickUp does not fit** if you want to trial client access on the free plan — on Free Forever, guests cannot be restricted, which is the opposite of what client work needs (details below) — or if you need proper timesheet approvals and unlimited billable-time features, which sit on higher plans.
- **Start on Core, not free, if clients will log in.** The reasons are in the guest section; they are the most important facts in this review.

### The "client portal" is something you assemble, not something you buy

ClickUp does not list a separate client portal product on its pricing page. What it offers is a set of building blocks, and ClickUp's own guide to creating a client portal assembles them like this:

- **One Folder per client** inside your Workspace, holding that client's projects, so client work is separated structurally from the start.
- **Docs** for the brief, contract, and project notes, shared with the client as part of the portal.
- **A Dashboard per client** — ClickUp's guide points to 50+ dashboard widgets, including time-tracking cards, so the client can see progress and logged hours without opening individual tasks.
- **Forms** for intake, so new requests arrive as structured tasks instead of emails. The free plan includes 1 Form; Core includes unlimited Forms (official pricing page).
- **Guest permissions** controlling exactly which Folders, Lists, tasks, and Docs each client can see.

That is a workable portal, and it costs nothing beyond the plan itself. The honest cost is your setup time: there is no one-click portal, no client branding on the plans a freelancer would buy (white labeling is an Enterprise feature on the pricing page), and each new client means repeating the Folder–Doc–Dashboard assembly. ClickUp also publishes client services templates to shorten that setup, per its guide.

### Guest rules: the section that decides whether this works

Guests are how clients get in, and ClickUp's guest system has three tiers of behaviour that matter more than any feature list. All of the following is from ClickUp's official pricing page FAQ and its Help Center article on guest-type user roles, checked November 27, 2026.

**On the Free Forever plan, guests cannot be restricted.** The pricing page states it plainly: on Free Forever, "guests have full create and edit permissions, which cannot be modified," and the Help Center confirms Free Forever guests get "full permissions only — no other permissions can be given." A client you invite on the free plan can edit what you share with them, and you cannot make them read-only. For client work, that rules the free plan out as a client portal, however generous the rest of it looks.

**On paid plans, there are two guest types:**

| Guest type | What they can do | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| View-only guest | See only the items and locations shared with them | Free, unlimited number on all paid plans |
| Permission-controlled guest | Per shared item: view only, comment, edit, or full edit (edit and delete) | Uses an included guest seat; beyond the included seats, each costs the same as a member subscription on your plan |

**Included permission-controlled guest seats:** Core includes 5 guest seats for a one-paid-user Workspace, plus 2 more for each additional paid user. Business includes 10, plus 5 per additional paid user. (The pricing page FAQ lists Core, Business, and Enterprise; it does not state a MAX-plan figure, so we do not quote one.)

Other guest rules worth knowing before you invite a real client:

- **Guests see only what you share.** Guests have no default access to your Workspace; they see the specific items and locations shared with them, and a guest cannot be invited to a whole Space — you share Folders, Lists, tasks, Docs, or Dashboards.
- **Your own team cannot be guests.** People who use your organisation's email domain or SSO cannot be added as guests; they become paid member-type users instead.
- **You can hide money talk.** Admins and owners can hide individual Custom Fields from all guests in the Workspace, so internal fields — your rate, your margin, your notes — stay internal even on a shared board.
- **Clients who only watch are free.** The practical setup for a designer: clients who just check progress are view-only guests (free, unlimited). The one person at the client who approves deliverables gets a permission-controlled seat with comment or edit rights on that client's Folder only.

### Time tracking: native, but read the limits table before you rely on it

Time tracking is built into ClickUp — it is a ClickApp the Workspace owner or admin enables — and entries live on the tasks themselves, which is exactly where a freelancer wants them. Entries can be marked billable or non-billable, and a Billable Report dashboard card exists for reviewing billable time (ClickUp Help Center).

The catch is in ClickUp's own Help Center table, "Time Tracking feature availability and limits" (checked November 27, 2026), which splits time tracking into basic and "advanced" actions:

| Time tracking capability | Free Forever | Core ($9) | Business ($17) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Create, edit, or delete a time entry | 100 uses | Unlimited | Unlimited |
| Advanced actions: mark entries billable, add descriptions or tags, edit entries from Timesheets, log time for other users, track time not attached to a task | 40 uses | **Trial** | 100 uses |
| Timesheet approvals (submit, request changes, approve) | Not included | Not included | 100 uses on existing plans; the Help Center notes approvals are **not included on new Business plans** |

Three consequences for a freelancer:

1. **On the free plan, time tracking is a sampler.** The pricing page itself labels the free plan's time tracking as "Time Tracking (Trial)," and the Help Center caps basic entries at 100 uses and billable-flagging at 40 uses. That is enough to evaluate, not enough to run a billing month.
2. **On Core, basic tracking is unlimited but the billable flag is listed as "Trial."** That is ClickUp's own wording in its limits table, and it is the single most important line in this review if you bill by the hour: the plan most freelancers would buy does not clearly include unlimited billable marking. ClickUp also notes that timesheet features and plan availability are subject to change and may be offered with unlimited usage on any plan for a limited time — so check the limits inside your own Workspace before a billing cycle depends on them.
3. **Timesheets as a named feature sit on the Business plan card** on the pricing page, and timesheet approvals are not part of Core at all.

None of this makes ClickUp's time tracking bad — native tracking on the task, with billable flags and a billable report, is the right shape for freelance work. It means the honest entry point for an hourly-billing freelancer may be Business at $17, not Core at $9, and you should verify the current Core trial terms in your Workspace before committing.

ClickUp's own Learn Hub page states the regular prices as the monthly-billing prices — Core $9 billed yearly or $12 billed monthly; Business $17 or $25 — so budget with the yearly figures only if you will actually pay yearly.

Billing facts from the pricing page FAQ that affect small teams:

- **Upgrading means upgrading the whole Workspace.** You cannot upgrade just your own seat; every member is upgraded, and payment is per Workspace — so a separate Workspace per client would mean a separate upgrade per client. One Workspace with one Folder per client is the cheaper structure.
- **AI is metered.** Paid plans include a pooled monthly AI credit allowance — 1,000 credits per seat on Core and 1,500 on Business, per ClickUp's Learn Hub page — and extra credits are sold separately ($10 per 10,000). For weekly status summaries this allowance is unlikely to be your constraint; for heavy AI use across many projects, price it in.
- **There is a 30-day refund guarantee.** The pricing page FAQ promises a full refund within 30 days if you are not satisfied, which lowers the risk of testing a paid plan with one real client project.

### Who ClickUp fits

- **Solo designers and 1–3 person studios** who want tasks, client visibility, and time records in one place, and who are willing to spend an afternoon assembling the per-client Folder/Doc/Dashboard structure once and reusing it.
- **Hourly billers on Business** who need billable flags, timesheets, and a billable report — with the plan limits above checked in their own Workspace first.
- **Project-fee designers on Core** who mainly need clients to *see* progress: view-only guests are free and unlimited, and that alone replaces most "just checking in" emails.

### Who should skip it

- **Anyone planning to invite clients on the free plan.** Unrestrict-able guest permissions are a confidentiality problem, not a minor limitation.
- **Anyone whose billing depends on timesheet approvals.** Approvals are absent from Free and Core, and ClickUp's Help Center says they are not included on new Business plans either.
- **Anyone who needs invoices from the same tool.** ClickUp's pricing page lists no invoicing feature; ClickUp records the time and the approvals, and the invoice itself still belongs in a dedicated invoicing tool.
- **Anyone who wants zero setup.** ClickUp's own Learn Hub review is candid that the platform rewards structured setup and that teams who skip it tend to struggle — the hierarchy (Workspace, Spaces, Folders, Lists, Tasks) is power you have to organise, not a template that organises itself.

### FAQ

**Can a client see my other clients' work?**
Not if you set it up as described: guests see only the items and locations shared with them, and they cannot be given a whole Space. Keep one Folder per client, share only that Folder, and hide internal Custom Fields from guests.

**Can I use ClickUp free as my client portal?**
Based on ClickUp's official documentation, we would not: free-plan guests receive full create and edit permissions that cannot be modified. The free plan is fine for evaluating ClickUp with your own work; client access should start on Core.

**Do my clients cost me seats?**
Clients who only view are free and unlimited on paid plans. Clients who need to comment or edit use permission-controlled guest seats — 5 included on Core for a solo Workspace, plus 2 per additional paid user — and beyond the included seats, each costs the same as a member subscription.

**Does ClickUp replace my invoicing tool?**
No. It gives you time records and approval trails to invoice *from*; the invoice itself is issued elsewhere.

**What does ClickUp cost for a solo designer, really?**
$9 per user/month billed yearly on Core ($108/year), or $17 ($204/year) on Business if you need the fuller time-tracking and timesheet features — before taxes, at the official US pricing page figures checked on November 27, 2026. View-only clients add nothing.

### Bottom line

For a freelance designer, ClickUp's value is concentrated in two documented facts: unlimited free view-only client access on paid plans, and native time tracking on the same tasks the client sees. Its risks are equally documented: free-plan guests cannot be restricted, the billable flag is a capped or trial "advanced" action below Business, and the portal is an assembly job, not a product. Start on Core with one client Folder, verify the time-tracking limits inside your Workspace, and move to Business only when the limits table — not the marketing page — tells you to.
